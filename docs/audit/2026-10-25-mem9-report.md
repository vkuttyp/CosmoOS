# MEM-9: dirty pages bounded at an eighth of RAM, and ENOSPC at the write, 2026-10-25

The third of v1.0.14's PRs. The brief: MEM-9 is in progress. Dirty
page-cache pages were bounded neither by memory nor by the pool's free
space. A write the pool cannot hold should be refused `ENOSPC` at the
write, and a writer past memory should be throttled, asleep, holding no
lock lockdep classes as held across I/O. The bound is a fraction chosen
once in the design, not tuned by a test, and a throttling design that
needs a new lock order is the owner's to approve first. None was needed.

## The design

vfs `design.md`, "Dirty pages, bounded" and "Refused at the write";
cosmofs `design.md`, "Space, charged at the write"; invariants V40, CF34,
and V19 restated.

**The bound.** Dirty pages of reclaimable mounts (every mount but
ramfs's, whose pages are its store) are counted machine-wide,
`pagecache_dirty_pages()`, and bounded at `pagecache_limit() /
PAGECACHE_DIRTY_DIVISOR`: half the cache limit, an eighth of RAM, which
is 32 MiB on a 256 MiB guest. A writer that finds the count at the bound
goes into `pagecache_throttle`. It writes its own file back
(`pagecache_sync` under the vnode lock, `file_sync`'s order). While the
count is still at the bound it sleeps on the `pagecache-dirty` wait
queue, holding no lock, until a page cleaned anywhere takes the count
under the bound or 100 ms pass. It does this at most fifty times (5 s).
Under the bound the throttle is one load.

**Where it is called is the rule.** `file_write` and `aio`'s write call
`file_throttle` before they take the open file's lock, and `file_pwrite`
calls it at its top. `file_pwrite_held` is the write for a caller that
already holds `f->lock` and throttled first. No lock is taken in a new
order. The sleep is a lockdep completion class (`pagecache-dirty`, L21):
each wake records the locks its waker holds, and a wait with any of them
held is reported as a cycle. A shared mapping's write fault is not
throttled, since it holds the address space's locks; its pages are
counted, and the next `write` bounds them.

**ENOSPC at the write.** `cfs_dirty_check`, which already charged the run
bound (CFS-32), charges space too: a page of a regular file may become
dirty while

    dirty pages of the mount + 1 + CFS_DIRTY_META(that) <= free blocks - reserve

with `CFS_DIRTY_META(n) = n / 512 + 4`, and is refused `-ENOSPC`
otherwise. The page cache then writes the vnode's pages back once and
asks again, as it does for `-EFBIG`. `file_pwrite` then commits once
through the vnode's `sync` and writes again, since blocks the open
transaction replaced are free only once it commits. Only a second
refusal is the writer's `ENOSPC`. A directory's blocks are not charged:
they come from the metadata class, reserve included, so a deletion on a
full pool still has a block to write.

**The data class's floor is half the reserve, not the reserve.** The
first version of the fix (`bcc53210`) charged the write against the
reserve and also kept write-back out of it, and its first matrix found
the gap between the two. The metadata a transaction takes after a page
is charged comes from the same free blocks (first fit takes a block
above the reserve before one in it). In `test-fill`'s last transaction,
792 data blocks and 7 metadata ones were taken against 799 blocks free
above the reserve when the pages were charged, with an allowance of 5:
two pages lost, `-ENOSPC`. `test-large` lost 16 pages the same way
(`vfs: 16 dirty page(s) of inode 2 on cosmofs lost: write-back failed
(-28)`, x86-64). The write is now charged against the whole reserve,
write-back may take the reserve's upper half, and the lower half stays
the metadata's.

## Failing tests first (`eb39579f`)

- **`pagecache-dirty-writer`** (V40), a self-test. Write-back is off and
  the cache limit is lowered to 256 pages, so the bound is 128. One
  writer of 1024 pages may never have more than the bound plus one
  write's pages dirty on its mount. On main all 1024 were dirty; with
  the fix, at most 128.
- **`test-fill`** (`tests/boot/fill_test.py`, CF34 and V40), a release
  boot of an installed system on a 256 MiB guest. Stage `writer`:
  512 MiB with no `sync`, nothing killed, the pool clean. Stage
  `enospc`: one `dd` with no `sync` fills the root and ends in
  `ENOSPC` with no write-back failing and no dirty page lost; spawns and
  `sync` still work after it, and `rm` gives the space back.
- `test-large` (CFS-32's) now fails on any `dirty page(s) ... lost`
  line. On CFS-32's branch it had lost 167 pages on x86-64 and 88 on
  AArch64 at its last, refused chunk (`-ENOSPC`); here it loses none.

`pagecache-dirty-bound` (2048 pages written under the lowered limit,
and a writer behind another file's dirty pages asleep until their
`sync` wakes it, with lockdep seeing the wait and the wake) came with the
fix (`513fa923`) as the throttle's coverage.

## What it costs: the 1 GiB encrypted write

`test-encrypt`'s slot-2 stage writes 1 GiB to the encrypted root with a
`sync` every 32 MiB and then reads it back after a reboot. It is the
longest write in the matrix, and the throttle's sleeps are a candidate
cost, so it was compared with main.

The read-back does not pass through the throttle, and in pair 1 it was
37 % faster on the branch while the write was 26 % slower: the host
moves both by that much. The host was not quiet during pair 1. Compiler
and `dotnet` processes took more than 50 % of a CPU in 53 of the
branch run's 124 samples and 32 of main's 107 (the sampler counts the
test's own builds with them), and the owner's Parallels VM took about
one CPU throughout. In pair 2 the read-backs agreed (241 and 219 s) and
the write was 38 % slower on the branch, but the host was not equally
quiet either: compiler or `dotnet` processes passed 50 % of a CPU in 1
of main's 91 samples and in 40 of the branch's 114, and QEMU averaged
91 % of a CPU against 77 %. In all three AArch64 comparisons the branch
wrote more slowly, and in both pairs it ran under more load, so timing
alone does not separate the two.

**The throttle never ran in that write.** The branch with one temporary
log line in `pagecache_throttle` past its fast path, printing the dirty
count, the writer's own write-back time, and the sleeps and their time
(not committed), ran `test-encrypt` without the B1-B4 tail
(`ENCRYPT_B1_B4=0`) on 2026-10-26, on a quiet host (3 of 46 samples busy,
QEMU at 94 % of a CPU). The installed disk's kernel held the line, and
the slot-2 boot printed 720 `INFO` lines and none of it: the 1 GiB write,
with a `sync` every 32 MiB, never reached the bound, so the throttle cost
it one load per write. It wrote 1 GiB in 871 s, against main's 876 s on
the quiet host of pair 2. The branch's slower writes were the host: the
same write took 726 s to 1290 s across this branch, CFS-32's and main,
and the read-back, which no change here touches, 211 s to 489 s.
